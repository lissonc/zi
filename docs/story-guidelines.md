# Story guidelines

How to write a mnemonic story for a Radix entry. These are the rules Claude Code
follows when it writes a batch of proposals, and the yardstick for approving or
rejecting one.

A story has one job: starting from the **keyword**, it must hand you every
**component** of the character, in a form you can see with your eyes closed.

## The rules

1. **Pictures, not definitions.** Every component appears as a physical thing
   doing or suffering something. If the story could be recited without seeing
   anything, it is a definition and will not stick.

2. **One fixed sense of the keyword.** Pick the single sense the keyword is
   meant in and build the scene on it. The keyword, or the act it names, is
   where the story lands, normally in its last words.

3. **Use each component under its own name.** Link it as `[[name]]` or
   `[[name|alias]]`, where the alias is one of the entry's listed primitive
   meanings (or a plural or verb form of it). Never slide to a neighbouring
   idea: a *child* is not an *infant*, a *cliff* is not a *cave*. The
   neighbouring idea is usually another component, and the two stories will
   blur.

4. **Nothing else may steal the scene.** Props are allowed, but no prop may be
   as striking as a component, and no prop may be something that is itself an
   entry in the library. One focal image per component, plus the keyword.

5. **Exaggerate one thing.** Size, number, absurdity, a small shock. Give the
   scene one point that can start moving by itself when the reader closes
   their eyes. Mild is forgettable; cruel or disgusting is not required.

6. **The simplest plot that holds.** One sentence, about 15–30 words. Try a few
   plots and keep the barest one that still names everything. A reader should
   be able to retell it after a single reading.

7. **Let position help where it can.** If it costs nothing, have the scene put
   things where the character has them: what is on top is above, what is
   enclosed is inside, what comes first on the left arrives first.

8. **Keep relatives apart.** Characters that share a component (抱, 抗, 批 …)
   must not share a plot. Vary the setting and the action so that one story
   cannot be mistaken for its neighbour.

9. **Own words only.** Stories are written fresh for this library. They are
   not taken or adapted from a textbook.

## A quick test before approving

- Close your eyes: is there a scene?
- Say the keyword: does the scene come back?
- Run through the scene: does every component turn up, under its own name?
- Is there anything in the scene you might later mistake for a component?

If any answer is no, reject it and say which. The reason goes into the next
request and the story is rewritten.
